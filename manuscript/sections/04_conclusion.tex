In this work, we systematically assessed the accuracy, representability, and finite-shot numerical stability of spin-resolved QSE across 28 organic molecules from Thiel's excited-state benchmark set. In the statevector limit, QSE generally provides an accurate description of the ground state, whereas the one-body operator pool is less reliable for low-lying excited states, with representability generally degrading as the active space is enlarged. This degradation is particularly pronounced for states with substantial multiconfigurational character, highlighting the limitations of restricting the QSE subspace to one-body excitations. The magnitude of these errors varies considerably across molecules and active spaces, reflecting the sensitivity of subspace representability to both the electronic character of the target state and the chosen orbital space. Among the reference ansatz considered, UCCSD provides a robust compromise between spectral accuracy, circuit complexity, and spin purity. These results motivate the use of richer operator pools, including higher-order excitations, particularly for larger active spaces and strongly multiconfigurational states.
The overlap spectrum further reveals that the numerical instability of QSE is largely associated with basis states carrying small weights in the generated subspace. Although these small-eigenvalue directions are highly susceptible to finite-shot perturbations, they can retain physically relevant spectral information and therefore cannot be discarded indiscriminately. We find that the appropriate regularization threshold depends systematically on both the number of shots and the active-space size. For the one-body QSE construction and measurement protocol considered here, maintaining approximately constant finite-shot accuracy results in an empirical total-shot requirement scaling as $\mathcal{O}\left(n_{\mathrm{orb}}^{11}\right)$. This steep scaling highlights a central practical challenge for direct QSE matrix estimation on near-term quantum hardware.
Importantly, our analysis also indicates that this measurement overhead need not represent the ultimate performance limit of QSE. The projected $\mathbf{H}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ matrices exhibit substantial internal structure and strong statistical correlations, suggesting that treating their matrix elements as independent quantities is unnecessarily inefficient. Structure-aware post-processing, correlated estimation, and joint reconstruction of the projected matrices may therefore offer routes to recovering the relevant spectral information from substantially fewer measurements. Taken together, these results show that the practical viability of QSE is governed by a balance between subspace expressivity, numerical recoverability, and measurement cost. These findings establish practical considerations for applying QSE to molecular excited states and motivate future work on richer operator manifolds and structure-aware measurement strategies for scalable quantum simulations of chemical systems.
