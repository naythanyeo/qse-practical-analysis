\section{Additional Computational Details}
\label{sec:si-methods}
This section provides additional computational and implementation details to support full reproducibility of the methods presented in the main text. 
The following subsections detail the spin-resolved CASCI root-selection procedure, wavefunction representation, construction of the QSE projected matrices, finite-shot sampling protocol, and determination of the numerically stable QSE subspace.
% ============================
% CASCI Root requesting
% ============================

\subsection{CASCI Root Computation}
\label{sec:si-casci-root-computation}
All CASCI calculations were separated by spin-projection sector $M_S$ by setting the number of active $\alpha$ and $\beta$ spin electrons to be 

$n_\alpha = \frac{n_e}{2} + M_S, n_\beta = \frac{n_e}{2} - M_S$

Singlet states were obtained from the $M_S=0$ sector, while triplet states were calculated in the $M_S=0, +1, -1$ sectors. For each active-space spin projection sector, the determinant-space dimension, and therefore the maximum number of available roots, was $\binom{n_{\mathrm{o}}}{n_\alpha} \binom{n_{\mathrm{o}}}{n_\beta}$. 

Where available, the lowest 11 singlet states (ground state + 10 excited states), and the lowest 10 triplet states (within each spin projection sector) were calculated. To ensure that the target number of states was obtained, three times the required number of CASCI roots were initially requested from the FCI solver, subject to the maximum dimension of the corresponding determinant space. 
The resulting roots were then filtered according to their $\Ssq$ expectation values, retaining only roots that satisfy $\left| \Ssq - S(S+1) \right| < 10^{-5}$ with $S=0$ for singlets and $S=1$ for triplets.

\subsection{Wavefunction Representation}
\label{sec:si-wavefunction-representation}
Throughout this study, wavefunctions were represented in a common active spin-orbital determinant basis by a state vector of length $\binom{2 n_{\mathrm{o}}}{n_{\mathrm{e}}}$. 
This vector includes all determinants with $n_e$ electrons distributed among the $2n_{\mathrm{o}}$ active spin-orbitals (irrespective of their spin projections).
Determinants were ordered lexicographically using an interleaved spin-orbital convention $(1\alpha, 1\beta, 2\alpha, 2\beta, \ldots)$.

PySCF represented each wavefunction as a two-dimensional CI coefficient matrix with dimensions $\binom{n_{\mathrm{o}}}{n_{\mathrm{\alpha}}}$ by $\binom{n_{\mathrm{o}}}{n_{\mathrm{\beta}}}$, corresponding to the $\alpha$ and $\beta$ electron occupation strings. These coefficients were embedded into the corresponding entries of the common state-vector representation.  During this conversion, the fermionic phase arising from the permutation between PySCF's separated $\alpha$ and $\beta$ electron ordering and the interleaved spin-orbital convention was included explicitly.

\subsection{Projected Hamiltonian and Overlap Matrix Evaluation}
\label{sec:si-projected-matrices}
To construct the projected QSE matrix elements, the fermionic QSE expansion operators and each target observable were mapped separately to Pauli operators using the Jordan-Wigner transformation. 
The projected observables were then constructed by multiplication in the Pauli representation. 
Terms with coefficients of magnitude smaller than $10^{-12}$ were discarded after the final product construction. 
The resulting Pauli-string expectation values were then evaluated either exactly from the optimised VQE states, $\left|\Psi_{\mathrm{VQE}}\right\rangle$ or estimated from sampled measurement outcomes (detailed in the next subsection).
For all calculations performed, only the diagonal and upper-triangular matrix elements were constructed and evaluated. Lower-triangular matrix terms were then reconstructed using Hermitian symmetry.

\subsection{Finite-Shot Sampling}
\label{sec:si-finite-shot-sampling}
For each finite-shot calculation, all unique Pauli strings contributing to the projected Hamiltonian and overlap matrices were collected into a single global measurement pool and partitioned using a largest-first greedy qubit-wise-commuting (QWC) grouping algorithm. 
For each group, basis-rotation gates were appended to the optimized VQE circuit so that all Pauli strings could be measured in the computational $Z$ basis. 
Hadamard gates were applied for $X$-basis measurements, while $S^{\dagger}$ followed by a Hadamard gate was used for $Y$-basis measurements.

Measurement outcomes were generated for each rotated statevector using NumPy's multinomial sampler. 
The resulting sampled frequencies were used to estimate all Pauli-string expectation values within the corresponding commuting group, from which the projected $\mathbf{H}$ and $\mathbf{S}$ matrices were reconstructed.

At each shot count, ten statistically independent sampling runs were performed. 
The projected matrices were solved independently for each run, and reported energy errors were computed from the mean of the ten resulting energies.
